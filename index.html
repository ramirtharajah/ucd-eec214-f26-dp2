Ladder 3:1 Switched Capacitor DC-DC Converter

Consider the 3:1 switched capacitor ladder converter shown in Figure 1 and analyzed in class. The objective of this design project is to choose appropriate values for WW and LL of the NMOS and PMOS power FETs (SW0\textrm{SW}_{0} through SW5\textrm{SW}_{5}), flying capacitors C1C_{1}, C2C_{2}, and C3C_{3}, and output (bypass) capacitance COUTC_{OUT}. Floating Driver

One challenge in implementing switched-capacitor DC-DC converters is driving the gates of the FET switches. A sufficient voltage swing must be applied to the gate to turn the transistor fully on and off, but the swing must also be referenced to the source terminal of the transistor. These combined requirements are typically met using floating drivers, which can be realized using CMOS inverters and capacitive bootstrap circuits. For this project, you can assume ideal floating drivers as shown in Figure 2. The driver is modeled as a two-stage CMOS inverter buffer chain with its own power supply DC voltage source VDRVi=1.8VV_{DRVi} = 1.8\ \textrm{V} and input stimulus source VϕjiV_{\phi ji}, where ii corresponds to the driver for the ithi\textrm{th} switch (ii from 0 to 5) and jj corresponds to the two phases ϕ0\phi0 and ϕ1\phi1. Choose appropriate widths and lengths for the transistors to maintain an approximate fanout of 4 (FO4) buffer ratio, i.e. once you determine the appropriate size (W/LW/L) for each power FET, normalize the power FET area to a minimum-sized inverter, then size the output buffer of the floating driver such that the power FET presents a 4×4\times load. Similarly, the predriver (first inverter) should be sized such that the output buffer presents a 4×4\times load to it. Assume a minimum-sized inverter has WP/WN=1350nm/450nmW_{P}/W_{N} = 1350\ \textrm{nm}/450\ \textrm{nm} and uses minimum channel lengths. Connect the supply nodes VDDiV_{DDi} and VSSiV_{SSi} to the appropriate nodes in the ladder converter to drive the switches effectively.

Ladder Converter Transient Simulations

Use the transient simulation capability in the ADE environment/OrCAD PSpice to simulate the startup response of your ladder converter across changes in the load current and temperature. Initialize all flying capacitors and the output capacitor to 0 V.

  • Load Currents: Simulate the transient response until the converter output reaches steady-state at three load currents (minimum ILDI_{LD}, 10 μ\muA, and maximum ILDI_{LD}). Verify that the DC output voltage error specification is met.

  • Temperatures: Assuming maximum ILDI_{LD}, simulate the the transient response until the converter output reaches steady-state at three temperatures (0 ∘^{\circ}C, 27 ∘^{\circ}C, and 85 ∘^{\circ}C). Verify that the DC output voltage error specification is met.

Plot the results of your transient simulations for checkoff and for inclusion in your report. Define the settling time as the time the transient requires to settle within ±1\pm1% of the final value and verify that the startup transient settling time specification is met.
